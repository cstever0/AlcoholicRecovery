## 1. Project Title and Overview

The **Cardiomyopathy Recovery Intelligence Platform (CRIP)** is an open, reproducible, multi-modal clinical AI research proof of concept developed to investigate recovery trajectories in non-ischemic cardiomyopathy associated with heavy alcohol exposure.

The platform bridges real cardiac magnetic resonance (CMR) imaging with synthetic clinical toxicology, genomics, and electronic health record (EHR) data to explore interpretable prediction of 6-month left ventricular systolic recovery.

```
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                    CRIP MULTIMODAL DOMAINS                                       |
+----------------------------------+----------------------------------+----------------------------+
| 🫀 Real Cardiac MRI (ACDC)       | 🍷 Toxicology Biomarkers (PEth)  | 🧬 Titin (*TTN*) Genetics  |
| - 100 Annotated Training Scans   | - Direct Phosphatidylethanol     | - ACMG-AMP 5-tier variants |
| - End-Diastole & End-Systole     | - 3-tier clinical risk bands     | - Pathogenic TTNtv vs VUS  |
```

## 2. Problem Statement

Alcoholic cardiomyopathy (ACM) represents a clinically recognized form of dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM) characterized by impaired left ventricular contractility and chamber dilation resulting from chronic heavy ethanol consumption.

In clinical practice, ACM exhibits a uniquely reversible disease trajectory: complete cessation of alcohol intake often leads to substantial structural reverse remodeling and functional systolic recovery. However, clinical response remains highly heterogeneous:
- A subset of patients achieves marked recovery ($\Delta \text{LVEF} \ge 10\%$).
- Others experience persistent systolic dysfunction, refractory heart failure, or life-threatening arrhythmias despite documented sobriety.
- Subjective patient recall of drinking history is frequently unreliable, necessitating objective direct biomarkers like Phosphatidylethanol (PEth).
- Underlying genetic mutations (notably truncating variants in the giant sarcomeric protein Titin, *TTNtv*) and irreversible myocardial replacement fibrosis (late gadolinium enhancement, LGE) profoundly blunt recovery potential.

CRIP was engineered to model this clinical problem by integrating real imaging with simulated genetic and toxicological determinants.

## 3. Research Objectives

CRIP was established around four foundational research goals:

1. **Real Cardiac MRI Automation:** Ingest and process real clinical CMR scans from the Automated Cardiac Diagnosis Challenge (ACDC 2017), calculate standardized physical chamber volumetrics, and train an automated deep learning segmentation network (MONAI 2D U-Net) to quantify Left Ventricular Ejection Fraction (LVEF) within $\pm 5.0$ percentage points of expert consensus.
2. **Multimodal Synthetic Clinical Cohort:** Synthesize a literature-grounded clinical population ($N=3,000$ patients) capturing the interplay between toxic alcohol biomarkers (PEth), Titin genetics, tissue fibrosis, and longitudinal LVEF change under sustained abstinence.
3. **Interpretable Recovery Prediction:** Build and audit transparent, non-black-box predictive models (regularized Logistic Regression) providing exact additive log-odds feature attributions and linear SHAP decompositions.
4. **Clinical Research Demonstration & Interoperability:** Deliver an interactive, clinician-facing multi-page web platform featuring hypothetical scenario exploration, research reporting (Markdown, HTML, JSON), and bidirectional mock HL7 FHIR R4 bundle interoperability.

## 5. Features and Capabilities

- **High-Throughput Medical Image I/O:** SimpleITK and NiBabel pipelines parsing 3D/4D NIfTI cardiac volumes, preserving physical millimeter spacing and affine coordinate geometries.
- **Automated Deep Learning Segmentation:** Pre-configured MONAI 2D U-Net architecture predicting RV cavity, myocardium, and LV cavity directly from short-axis cine slices.
- **Physical Cardiac Volumetric Engine:** Geometric slice-by-slice voxel volume integration calculating LVEDV, LVESV, LVSV, LVEF, RVEDV, RVESV, RVSV, RVEF, and myocardial mass using the standard human myocardial tissue density constant ($\rho = 1.05\text{ g/mL}$).
- **Literature-Grounded Synthetic Clinical Database:** 3,000-subject synthetic multimodal database adhering to strict Pydantic clinical constraints ($ESV < EDV$, PEth risk bands, ACMG-AMP Titin classification).
- **Interpretable Machine Learning:** Scikit-learn recovery classification pipeline (`v1.0-m3b`) with exact additive log-odds decompositions ($\beta_j \times x_j$) and SHAP interpretability.
- **Interactive Multi-Page Streamlit Interface:** Modern 6-page UI covering platform overview, patient exploration, cine MRI segmentation inspection, dedicated biventricular cardiac measurements, multimodal recovery estimation, and scientific model evaluation.
- **Statistical Method Comparison & Bland–Altman Analysis:** Formal measurement agreement module (`crip.evaluation.measurement_agreement`) calculating mean bias, 95% limits of agreement, standard errors, and confidence intervals alongside interactive identity-line scatter plots ($y = x$).
- **Poster-Ready Figures & Artifacts:** Standalone generator exporting 8 publication-quality 300-DPI exhibits (PNG, SVG, PDF) with full provenance manifest.
- **Mock HL7 FHIR R4 Interoperability:** Bidirectional export, import, and schema validation of standard FHIR R4 bundles covering `Patient`, `Observation` (LOINC-coded), and `DiagnosticReport` resources.
- **Rigorous Automated Testing:** 296 automated pytest unit, integration, and regression tests ensuring zero mathematical contradictions, zero data leakage, and verified reproducible metrics.

## 10. Path A — Quick Start: Synthetic Data Only

> [!TIP]
> ### Two Independent Pathways to Reproduce CRIP
> - **PATH A (Quick Start: Synthetic Data Only):** Takes **3 to 5 minutes** from cloning to a fully interactive clinical dashboard. **No medical imaging downloads or challenge accounts required.** It uses deterministic synthetic clinical cohort generation ($N=3,000$ patients) and Scikit-learn recovery modeling. Fully powers the Streamlit Dashboard, Cohort Explorer, Recovery Estimator, and Evaluation metrics out of the box.
> - **PATH B (Complete MRI Multi-Dataset & Model Reproduction):** For researchers wishing to train or evaluate deep learning 2D U-Net models on **314 real cardiac MRI scans** across **ACDC 2017**, **M&Ms 2020**, and **EMIDEC 2020** (described in [Section 12](#12-path-b--complete-real-mri-dataset-acquisition-and-setup)).

Path A is completely self-contained and independently executable. Follow these step-by-step instructions:

### 10.1 Quick Start Prerequisites
- **Operating System:** macOS (Apple Silicon or Intel), Ubuntu Linux 22.04+, Ubuntu on WSL2, or Windows 10/11.
- **Python:** Version **3.11**, **3.12**, or **3.13** (tested through Python 3.13.15).
- **Git:** Version 2.25+.
- **Disk Space:** ~1.5 GB for the virtual environment, dependencies, and generated synthetic databases (no large MRI datasets needed).
- **RAM:** Minimum 8 GB.

---

### 10.2 Clone Repository & Setup Virtual Environment

Clone the official repository remote:

```bash
# Clone the repository
git clone https://github.com/cstever0/AlcoholicRecovery.git
cd AlcoholicRecovery
```

Create and activate an isolated Python virtual environment:

#### On macOS, Ubuntu Linux, or Ubuntu on WSL2:
```bash
python3 -m venv .venv
source .venv/bin/activate
```

#### On Windows (PowerShell):
```powershell
# Set execution policy for the current process if script activation is restricted:
Set-ExecutionPolicy -Scope Process -ExecutionPolicy Bypass
py -3 -m venv .venv
.venv\Scripts\Activate.ps1
```

Confirm that the virtual environment is active:
```bash
# Your prompt should show (.venv)
python --version
# Should output: Python 3.11.x, 3.12.x, or 3.13.x
```

### 10.4 Synthetic Cohort Generation

Generate the literature-grounded synthetic clinical database ($N=3,000$ patients) using fixed random seed 42:

```bash
python -m crip.synthetic.cohort_generator --n-patients 3000 --seed 42 --abstinence-prob 0.70 --output-dir data/synthetic
```

**Generated Artifacts in `data/synthetic/`:**
- `synthetic_cardiomyopathy_cohort.csv`: Full tabular cohort with 3,000 synthetic patient records.

#### 12.3.1 M&Ms Archive Extraction, Pairing & Ubuntu/WSL Setup

When unpacking or syncing the M&Ms 2020 dataset:

1. **Paired Ground-Truth Annotations**:
   - The challenge distributes 345 total study directories. Of these, exactly **114 studies** possess expert ground-truth masks with complete End-Diastolic (ED) and End-Systolic (ES) frame indices:
     - **80 paired studies** in `Training/Labeled/` (split into 60 model training and 20 validation cases).
     - **34 paired studies** in `Validation/` (held out as the zero-leakage evaluation cohort).
   - The remaining 231 directories represent unannotated challenge cases, mask-only transfers, or testing partitions.
2. **Canonical Split Manifest (`docs/unified/cohort_splits.json`)**:
   - Patient split assignments are version-controlled in `docs/unified/cohort_splits.json`.
   - On a fresh clone on Ubuntu/WSL, CRIP automatically initializes the processed split manifest from this canonical reference, guaranteeing that expected cohort counts (60 train, 20 val, 34 holdout) remain immutable.
3. **Cross-Platform Extraction Best Practices (Ubuntu / WSL2)**:
   - **Directory Structure:** Ensure archives are unpacked into `data/raw/mnms/` matching `Training/Labeled/` and `Validation/`. If an extraction utility creates a wrapper directory (e.g. `data/raw/mnms/OpenDataset/`), CRIP's loader automatically detects it, but moving folders directly under `data/raw/mnms/` is recommended.
   - **Case Sensitivity:** Linux ext4 is case-sensitive. Filenames are standardized as `{CASE_ID}_sa.nii.gz` (cine volume) and `{CASE_ID}_sa_gt.nii.gz` (ground truth). CRIP handles case-insensitive file variants gracefully.
   - **Preflight Verification:** Always execute the dry-run preflight check before attempting joint training:
     ```bash
     python -m crip.pipeline train --task cine --dry-run
     ```
     This validates physical case discovery, indexed slice counts (1,100 ACDC train, 404 ACDC val; 1,300 M&Ms train, 434 M&Ms val), and holdout quarantine without modifying any trained checkpoints.

#### 12.3.2 Windows/WSL NTFS Alternate Data Stream Metadata Cleanup

When downloading or extracting archives under Windows or WSL, NTFS Alternate Data Streams (such as `:Zone.Identifier` files) may be attached to unpacked NIfTI files. These metadata files can interfere with directory scanning, case pairing, and globbing. Clean them up with this quick command from the repository root:

```bash
find data/ -name "*:Zone.Identifier" -delete
```

## 22. Git Research Provenance & Workflow Isolation

When reproducing research experiments across different computers or operating systems (such as migrating from macOS Apple Silicon to Ubuntu on WSL2), managing Git working trees and research artifacts requires care.

### 22.1 Understanding Tracked Output Modifications

The CRIP repository tracks reference scientific figures (`docs/evaluation/**/*.png`, `*.svg`, `*.pdf`) and experiment training metadata (`docs/evaluation/**/*.json`) to provide immediate documentation and visualization without requiring every reader to download gigabytes of raw MRI scans.

When you execute model training, evaluation, or figure generation scripts on your local computer, the scripts recompute metrics and re-export figures to these directories:
- **Numerical Floating-Point Variations:** Even with fixed random seeds (`seed=42`), minor differences in hardware floating-point math (e.g., Apple Silicon Metal Performance Shaders vs. NVIDIA CUDA vs. x86_64 CPU vectorization) will produce infinitesimal differences in neural network gradients, loss histories, and Matplotlib vector anti-aliasing.
- **Git Status Diffs:** Consequently, running `python -m crip.pipeline train --task recovery` or figure generation scripts will cause Git to report tracked figure files and summary JSONs as modified (`git status`).
- **This is expected behavior, not a corruption error.**

### 22.2 Best Practices for Experiment Isolation & Preservation

To maintain clean repository hygiene while reproducing results:

1. **Work on a Dedicated Reproduction Branch:**
   Never run full reproduction pipelines directly on `main` if you plan to commit code modifications:
   ```bash
   git checkout -b reproduction-run
   ```
2. **Inspect Changes with Git Status & Git Diff:**
   Periodically check what files have been regenerated:
   ```bash
   git status
   git diff --stat docs/evaluation/
   ```
3. **Distinguish Source Code from Generated Assets:**
   - **Source Code (`src/crip/`, `tests/`):** Should only change when fixing bugs or enhancing algorithms.
   - **Local Generated Data (`data/processed/`, `data/synthetic/`):** Excluded from Git via `.gitignore`.
   - **Trained Model Weights (`models/mri/*.pt`, `models/multidataset/*.pt`):** Excluded from Git.
   - **Tracked Research Assets (`docs/evaluation/`):** Reference baselines published with the paper/milestone.
4. **Never Use Destructive Reset Commands as Routine Cleanup:**
   > [!CAUTION]
   > Do **NOT** run `git reset --hard` or `git clean -fdx` blindly. Doing so will delete your downloaded raw MRI datasets (`data/raw/`), local virtual environments (`.venv/`), and freshly trained model checkpoints (`models/`).
5. **Preserving Local Checkpoints & Outputs:**
   If you wish to preserve existing reference figures while testing new training runs, make a local backup of `docs/evaluation/` before executing training:
   ```bash
   cp -r docs/evaluation docs/evaluation_reference_backup
   ```

### 27.4 EMIDEC Delayed-Enhancement Tissue Characterization Benchmark

CRIP provides full support for delayed-enhancement (late gadolinium enhancement, LGE) CMR tissue characterization using the **EMIDEC** dataset (100 training cases: 33 Normal Controls, 67 Pathological Infarction Cases). 

#### Mathematical Formulation:
- **Voxel Volume:** $V_{\text{voxel}} = \frac{dx \cdot dy \cdot dz}{1000.0} \text{ mL}$
- **Total Myocardium:** $V_{\text{total\_myo}} = V_{\text{norm\_myo}} + V_{\text{infarct}} + V_{\text{no\_reflow}}$ (excludes LV blood pool)
- **Infarction Burden:** $\text{Burden}_{\text{infarct}} = \frac{V_{\text{infarct}}}{V_{\text{total\_myo}}} \times 100\%$
- **No-Reflow (MVO) Burden:** $\text{Burden}_{\text{mvo}} = \frac{V_{\text{no\_reflow}}}{V_{\text{total\_myo}}} \times 100\%$
- **Myocardial Mass:** $M_{\text{myo}} = V_{\text{total\_myo}} \times 1.05 \text{ g/mL}$

#### Clinical Governance Invariants:

EMIDEC tissue characterization serves as a viability quantification benchmark and must never be conflated with ACM fibrosis.

- **Error:** `File Activate.ps1 cannot be loaded because running scripts is disabled on this system.`
- **Cause:** Default Windows PowerShell security policy restricts unsigned script execution.
- **Remedy:** Allow script execution for the active terminal process:
  ```powershell
  Set-ExecutionPolicy -Scope Process -ExecutionPolicy Bypass
  .venv\Scripts\Activate.ps1
  ```

#### 3. Unsupported Python Version (< 3.11)
- **Error:** `ERROR: Package 'crip' requires a different Python: 3.10.x not in '>=3.11'`
- **Cause:** CRIP requires Python 3.11, 3.12, or 3.13 due to modern typing syntax and dependency specifications.
- **Remedy:** Install Python 3.12 via Homebrew (`brew install python@3.12`), apt (`sudo apt-get install python3.12 python3.12-venv`), or python.org, then recreate `.venv`:
  ```bash
  rm -rf .venv
  python3.12 -m venv .venv
  source .venv/bin/activate
  pip install -r requirements.txt
  pip install -e .
  ```

#### 4. Matplotlib Font Cache Warnings or Permission Errors
- **Notice:** `Matplotlib is building the font cache; this may take a moment` or `Path /Users/<user>/.matplotlib is not writable`.
- **Cause:** Matplotlib attempts to write its font cache to the user home directory, which may have restricted permissions in certain sandboxed or automated environments.
- **Remedy:** Point Matplotlib to a temporary writable directory:
  ```bash
  export MPLCONFIGDIR=/tmp/matplotlib
  ```

### 28.2 Dataset Acquisition and Organization Problems

#### 1. Missing ACDC Dataset in Path A vs. Path B
- **Behavior:** In Path A (Synthetic Quick Start), Pages 1, 3, and 4 work completely. Pages 2 and 5 display an informative notice indicating that raw CMR scans have not been downloaded. **The application does not crash.**

#### 2. Windows/WSL NTFS Alternate Data Stream Metadata (`*:Zone.Identifier`)
- **Symptoms:** Globbing mismatches, unexpected filenames like `patient001_frame01.nii.gz:Zone.Identifier`, or SimpleITK reader errors on WSL2.
- **Cause:** Downloading archives via Windows web browsers or extracting files across NTFS partitions attaches Windows Security Zone metadata streams.
- **Remedy:**
  ```bash
  # 1. Review affected metadata files (diagnostic check):
  find data/raw -type f -name '*:Zone.Identifier' -print

  # 2. Safely remove only the Zone.Identifier metadata sidecars:
  find data/raw -type f -name '*:Zone.Identifier' -delete
  ```
  *(Never delete actual `.nii.gz` image volumes or ground-truth masks).*

#### 3. Case Sensitivity and Nested Extraction Folders
- **Symptoms:** `FileNotFoundError: Expected patient directory patient001 not found.`
- **Cause:** Archive extraction utilities occasionally create nested wrapper directories (e.g. `data/raw/acdc/training/training/patient001` or `data/raw/mnms/OpenDataset/Training/`).
- **Remedy:** Verify your directory layout against Section 12.3. Ensure patient folders reside directly under `data/raw/acdc/training/patientXXX` and M&Ms folders reside under `data/raw/mnms/Training/Labeled/`.

- **Historical Error:** `AttributeError: 'str' object has no attribute 'get'` in `src/crip/app/pages/4_Evaluation.py` at line 603 (`pred_p = [b.get("mean_predicted_prob", 0) for b in cc]`).
- **Verified Root Cause:** The multi-dataset recovery pipeline (`src/crip/pipeline/recovery_trainer.py`) serializes calibration curves into `data/processed/multidataset/synthetic_recovery_comparison.json` as a dictionary of parallel coordinate arrays (`{"mean_predicted": [...], "fraction_positives": [...]}`). - **Corrected Behavior:** Implemented `extract_calibration_curve_points()` in `src/crip/evaluation/model_comparison.py` to robustly detect and normalize both dictionary-of-arrays format (`mean_predicted`, `fraction_positives`) and historical list-of-dicts format (`mean_predicted_prob`, `observed_frequency`). Missing values are never fabricated or defaulted to zero.
- **Regenerating Artifacts (Optional):** If existing benchmark files are intact, regeneration is NOT required. To re-run recovery benchmarks if needed:
  ```bash
  python -m crip.pipeline train --task recovery
  ```


  Open `http://localhost:8501`, navigate to **Model Evaluation (Page 4) $\to$ Tab 3: Recovery Prediction Benchmarks $\to$ Select "Experiment 2: Unselected All-Comers Cohort"** to confirm calibration curves and comparative metrics render cleanly.

## 29. Reproducibility and Limitations

### Computational Reproducibility
- All synthetic cohort generation, dataset splitting, U-Net training, and Scikit-learn pipelines use fixed seeds (seed 42).
- Minor variations in floating-point outputs may arise across different hardware backends (e.g., Apple Silicon MPS vs. NVIDIA CUDA vs. CPU vectorization).

### Scientific and Clinical Limitations
1. **Synthetic Outcomes:** Recovery endpoints ($\Delta \text{LVEF} \ge 10.0\%$) and biomarker concentrations are generated computationally from published empirical distributions. They do not constitute real clinical prospective data.
2. **ACDC Cohort Scope:** The public ACDC dataset contains cases of dilated cardiomyopathy, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, myocardial infarction, and right ventricular abnormalities. It does not contain clinically verified alcoholic cardiomyopathy cases.
3. **No Clinical Reference Consensus:** All ACDC volume measurements are calculated via geometric voxel integration of expert segmentation masks. The challenge did not provide separate core-lab tabulated consensus scalar values.
